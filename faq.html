Frequently Asked Questions

Questions I often get asked by undergraduates

How did you know you wanted to go to graduate school?

I was beyond lucky to have an amazing mentor in undergrad (Dr. Jason Strickland). He really showed me how cool biology was, and how much remains unanswered. He pushed me to ask hard questions and find answers, which made me fall in love with all the good, bad, and ugly parts of research. I was lucky enough to get a lot of research experience and have independence and ownership of my project, this further deepened my love for research.

For me, I knew I wanted to go to graduate school because I had so many more questions I wanted to answer. I truly love research and learning, and what better way then to spend 5 years deep diving into a topic I find fascinating! I am lucky to say I truly love my job and am excited I get to do this every day, even when it is very hard.

Read about my work on snake venom here!

Do I have to do research in undergraduate to go to graduate school?

It is not necessarily a requirement. However, I can not express enough how my undergraduate project led me down the path I am on. It made me a more critical thinker, gave me a sense of confidence, and taught me how to make mistakes and learn from them. Do not let a “lack” of experience prevent you from applying though. I think excitement/curiosity plays a much bigger role in graduate school acceptance than how many gels you ran.

Should I take a gap year?

This is a very personal decision. You have to ask yourself, what would a gap year do for you? Do you need a breather from school? Do you want to gain more experience? Do you want to explore other disciplines before deciding a Ph.D.focus? Are these benefits more important than starting grad school? Just know, no matter how much time you take off or experience you have beforehand, grad school is still going to be scary and you will still feel like you do not know what you are doing. I do not think any one “feels ready” for grad school, sometimes you just gotta take the leap and go for it.

Even if you know you are taking a gap year, I recommend applying to graduate schools. They are difficult to get into and practice applications/interviews will come in handy when/if you apply after a gap year.

Do I have to know what I want to study before grad school?

Not necessarily, however, I do think you should have a strong interest in a broad field. For me I was interested in disease. I found myself fascinated by the complexity of pathogen-host systems and the transmission process. However, I did not know any specific question or study systems that would be particularly interesting to me. Focus on figuring out questions you find interesting, not so much what system to study them in. Quite a few graduate schools actually do rotations. In your first semester/year you do not affiliate with a lab and rather bounce around, spending 8-12 weeks in a lab on a project. Then at the end of your first year you affiliate. THIS IS NOT THE CASE FOR ALL PROGRAMS. Some programs you hire straight into a lab and don’t switch. These are important to think about when applying too schools.

How do I find out what I want to study?

Become disgustingly well read. Read everything across multiple field/disciplines. Let yourself nerd out and be fascinated by this crazy cool world we live in!
