Finding a Graduate Advisor

Everything you need to know about finding the right graduate mentor.

Finding a field of interest

The best way for me to find faculty I wanted to work with was to identify some fields of interest a nd determine the leaders in that field. Then READ READ READ. Find papers that fascinate you and generate a desire to learn more.

Emailing faculty members

Once you identify a topic of interest, start reaching out to faculty members via email. I've included a template email and a real example of an email I sent. Check faculty websites for active projects and whether they are recruiting graduate students. Read multiple papers by the faculty member and familiarize yourself with current projects in their lab. Be personal, show genuine interest, and be polite.

Template for emailing faculty

Remember, many faculty members may say no or simply not respond. This isn't personal, so don't let it dampen your spirits!

Ideally, after a faculty member responds, you'll have an opportunity to meet over Zoom. Always ask to speak with current graduate students in their lab if the faculty member doesn't offer. Ask students about their research projects, mentorship style, and experiences at the university. These conversations can give you valuable insight into what working with that advisor is actually like.

ECOLOG

I also highly reccomend signing up for ECOLOG! ECOLOG is an email list where faculty members post opportunities for prospective graduate students. It's a great way to discover labs that are actively recruiting students.
